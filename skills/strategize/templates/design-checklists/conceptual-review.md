# Design Checklist: Conceptual Review (structured literature review + framework-based synthesis)

Use when the contribution is an argument built from an assembled body of literature read
through an explicit analytical framework — no primary data, no estimation, no formal model.
(Formal theory with proofs is `/strategize theory`; this is not that.)

## Memo section mapping (the five required headings keep their names)
- **Estimand** → the target claim: the one-sentence answer to the research question, and its scope.
- **Specification** → search sources and strings, date range, inclusion/exclusion rules,
  corpus counts (assembled vs analysed), the framework, the coding procedure.
- **Assumptions** → what must hold for the framework to be a fair lens: origin context,
  what it can and cannot see, why the coded subset represents the assembled corpus.
- **Robustness Plan** → sensitivity of findings to corpus scope, to the framework choice,
  and to coder judgement.
- **Threats** → rival readings of the synthesis, including "the finding is an artifact of the lens".

## Argument architecture (the story arc) — required section in the memo
- [ ] Research question stated once, early, and answered in the conclusion in the same terms.
- [ ] Claim chain: question → why existing work cannot answer it → design → framework →
      layer findings → cross-layer finding → contribution; each link names its section.
- [ ] Reverse outline: one line per manuscript paragraph (argument move + claim); flag any
      paragraph with no move, two moves, or a move out of arc order.
- [ ] Every finding in the abstract appears in the analysis, and vice versa.
- [ ] Contribution stated as what the reader can now do or see, not as coverage.
- [ ] Background defines every term the analysis uses before the analysis uses it.
- [ ] The paper's own propositions are labelled separately from synthesised findings.
- [ ] No discursive passage: every paragraph advances the claim chain.

## Referee objections to anticipate
- "This is a literature review, not research" — the design and coding make it replicable.
- "The framework produced the finding" — the cross-layer interdependence is the finding.
- "Why this framework?" — selection rationale and what an alternative lens would change.
